- **Parser**: function arguments, the SELECT list, GROUP BY and ORDER BY are now collected on parser-owned scratch stacks and copied once into exact-size slices (previously grown with `append` from a fixed capacity). A `FunctionCall` with up to three arguments shares one allocation with its argument array, and a `SelectStatement` shares one with its single `From` entry and, for up to four columns, its `Columns` array. Parse allocations drop 28% on `aggregate` (18 to 13) and 12% on `complex` (50 to 44), and allocs/op fall on every SELECT-shaped parse row (INSERT, UPDATE, DELETE, tokenizer-error paths and the pool and tokenizer-only benchmarks are unchanged, as they never reach the list builders). In the layout-randomized amd64 CI perf gate (20 layouts per arm, AMD EPYC 7763) the suite geomean moved -0.79% (p=0.004), the parse aggregate -3.24% (p=0.005, not significant after Benjamini-Hochberg correction, low power) and `complex` -1.35% (not significant); instructions/op fell 3.48% on the aggregate and 1.13% on `complex`, and allocs/op on the end-to-end parse fell from 18 to 13 (aggregate) and from 49 to 43 (complex). No gated benchmark regressed in time. User-visible: `Columns`, `GroupBy`, `OrderBy` and `FunctionCall.Arguments` now have `len == cap` (they used to carry spare capacity), so a caller that appends to them reallocates instead of writing into spare space; trees are otherwise identical (guarded by the behaviour goldens). Lifetime trade-off: because the blocks are co-allocated, retaining only `Columns`, `From` or `Arguments` of a released tree keeps the whole block (up to 600 B) alive. No exported API change. [W4-04]
